02Marks and details

the littlegraveyard bits.

The pieces that make a header feel like ours: the arch, a strip of tape, a brushy underline, a shoot coming through. Use them lightly, one or two per piece, never all at once.

The arch

A thin headstone outline. Frames the crow in the logo and can frame a drawing or an epitaph line. Hairline only, never filled with a heavy colour.

notes from the morgue

The tape

Torn Lichen tape with a word or two in marker. Labels a section: Essays, Notes from the Morgue, Obsolete Concepts, Epitaphs.

The brush stroke

One dry brush line under or between the headline and the script line. Lichen, New Shoot or Dried Rose. One per piece.

The sign-off

“half thoughts and noble nonsense.” with @DEADIDEASPROJECT underneath, centred at the bottom of every header.

The DIP on top

Small and centred at the top of a header, like a letterhead. It tells people whose post it is before they read a word.

Shoots and ribbons

Every drawing has a little life in it: a sage shoot, and sometimes one red bookmark ribbon. That’s the only red in a drawing.

03Colour

graveyard,but warm.

Ink and Paper do most of the work, with Parchment for softer pieces and Yew Green when we go dark. Lichen, New Shoot and Dried Rose are accents: the tape, the brush stroke, a shoot. Tap any code to copy it. Print codes are a starting point, so always ask the printer for a proof.

How much of each

A rough recipe for a header, post or page. Lots of pale space, black type doing the talking, and one accent showing up once.

Which stroke, when

The brush stroke is where the colour lives. Pick it by the mood of the post.

Yes. No. Maybe.

Lichen is the everyday one. Gentle, thinking-out-loud posts.

What makes an idea worth recording?

New Shoot is for ideas coming back to life, invitations and anything hopeful.

How many is too many

Dried Rose is for the sharper ones: frustration, honesty, a bit of swearing.

Pairings that work

Checked for readability, not guessed. Ink reads on every light colour; Parchment reads on Yew Green, Ink and Dried Rose.

04Type

three fonts,three jobs.

League Gothic says the thing. Soli Px mutters the aside. Canva Sans explains. Pick the font by the job the words are doing.

Sentence case

Headlines read like a thought: “How many is too many”, not “HOW MANY IS TOO MANY”. Capitals are for the logo.

One aside

One Soli Px line per piece, under or beside the headline. It’s the muttered bit, so keep it short.

Stack it tall

League Gothic is narrow, so let it run big and tight across two to four lines. Ten words is plenty.

No fourth font

No extra Canva fonts, no gothic blackletter, no typewriter. The drawings bring the character.

the permission slip

Most people have a graveyard of ideas they’ve never mentioned to anyone: notebooks half full, voice memos never replayed, ideas crossed out and rewritten on the next page.

This isn’t a business start-up resource, productivity advice, or a course that helps you find your one winning idea and execute it. It’s a permission slip.

“Collecting dead ideas isn’t a sign of failure. It’s proof you’re creative, curious, and alive.”

Before it goes out

Does it sound like Daniela talking, not a brand talking? Is there one moment the reader goes “that’s me”? And does it end by naming the pattern, the small nod towards saving your own ideas, rather than just the joke?

06Illustration

pen, ink,and a little bit of life.

We don’t use photos. Every post gets an engraved pen-and-ink drawing: one object telling the story, cross-hatched in black, a touch of sage, a shoot growing through it. Tap any drawing to download it with a see-through background.

Draw this

  • Notebooks, journals, letters, signposts, suitcases, benches: things ideas live in
  • Fine engraving and cross-hatching, black ink on white
  • Muted sage on the odd tape, leaf or label
  • A shoot coming through somewhere, always
  • One red bookmark ribbon at most
  • A patch of soil and ink speckle grounding it

Skip this

  • Stock photos, or any photo at all
  • Skulls, blood, cobwebs and horror-film gothic
  • Bright full colour, flat vector or cute cartoon
  • People as the hero (a tiny figure in a picture is fine)
  • Background scenes or boxes behind the drawing
  • Real logos or readable brand names

Making a new one?

Hand-drawn pen-and-ink engraving of [one object that tells the post’s story], fine cross-hatching, black ink on a plain white background, small touches of muted sage green (#a5ab8a), one deep red bookmark ribbon, a small seedling sprouting from it, a patch of soil and ink speckle at the base, no frame, no background scene, no text.
